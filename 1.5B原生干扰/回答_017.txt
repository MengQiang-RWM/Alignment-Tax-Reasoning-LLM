Let's break down the problem step by step:

1. **Initial Apples:**
   - Xiao Ming starts with 3 apples.

2. **Apples After Exchange:**
   - Xiao Hua gives 1 apple to Xiao Ming.
   - So after this exchange, Xiao Ming has \(3 + 1 = 4\) apples.

3. **Relation Between Xiao Hua and Xiao Hong:**
   - Xiao Hua has half as many apples as Xiao Hong.
   - Let \(H\) represent the number of apples Xiao Hua has after giving one to Xiao Ming.
   - Let \(X\) represent the number of apples Xiao Hong has initially (which is equal to the initial number of apples Xiao Ming had, which was 3).
   - According to the information given, \(H = \frac{1}{2}X\).

4. **Apples Xiao Hong Has Now:**
   - Since Xiao Hua has \(H\) apples after giving one to Xiao Ming, we can write:
     \[
     H = X - 1
     \]
   - Substituting \(X\) for the initial number of apples Xiao Ming had:
     \[
     H = 3 - 1 = 2
     \]

5. **Comparison between Xiao Hong and Xiao Ming:**
   - Xiao Hong carries a backpack with 2 more apples than Xiao Ming.
   - Let \(M\) represent the number of apples Xiao Ming has now.
   - Let \(B\) represent the number of apples Xiao Hong has now.
   - We know that \(B = M + 2\).

6. **Solving for \(M\):**
   - From the previous steps, we found that \(H = 2\) and \(X = 3\).
   - Therefore, Xiao Hua originally had 3 apples before giving one away, so \(X = 3\).
   - Thus, Xiao Hong originally had \(X = 3\) apples.
   - And since Xiao Hong has 2 more apples than Xiao Ming now:
     \[
     B = M + 2
     \]
   - Since \(B = 3 + 2 = 5\) (as calculated from the comparison):
     \[
     5 = M + 2
     \]
   - Solving for \(M\):
     \[
     M = 5 - 2 = 3
     \]

So, Xiao Ming still has 3 apples.
