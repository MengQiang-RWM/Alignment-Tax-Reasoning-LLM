Let's break down the problem step by step:

1. **Initial Number of Apples:**
   - Xiao Ming has 3 apples.
   - Xiao Hong has 2 more apples than Xiao Ming.
     \[
     \text{Number of apples Xiao Hong has} = 3 + 2 = 5
     \]
   
2. **Apples Given to Xiao Ming:**
   - Xiao Hua has half as many apples as Xiao Hong.
     \[
     \text{Number of apples Xiao Hua has} = \frac{5}{2}
     \]
     Since we cannot give half an apple in practical terms, let’s assume Xiao Hua has \( \frac{5}{2} \) apples (which is not possible with whole apples).

3. **Total Apples After Giving One Apple:**
   - Xiao Hua gives 1 apple to Xiao Ming.
   - Total apples Xiao Ming has after receiving one apple:
     \[
     \text{New number of apples Xiao Ming has} = 3 + 1 = 4
     \]

Therefore, after giving 1 apple to Xiao Ming, Xiao Ming has 4 apples. The reasoning here is that since Xiao Hong already had 5 apples and gave one to Xiao Ming, Xiao Ming ends up having only 4 apples due to the initial difference between Xiao Hong and Xiao Ming.
