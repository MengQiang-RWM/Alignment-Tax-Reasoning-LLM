Let's denote the number of apples Xiao Ming originally had as \( M \), the number of apples Xiao Hua originally had as \( H \), and the number of apples Xiao Hong originally had as \( R \).

From the problem, we know:
1. Xiao Ming initially has 3 apples: \( M = 3 \).
2. Xiao Hua gives 1 apple to Xiao Ming. So, Xiao Hua has \( H - 1 \) apples.
3. Xiao Hua has half as many apples as Xiao Hong: \( H - 1 = \frac{1}{2}R \).
4. Xiao Hong carries a cute backpack with 2 more apples than Xiao Ming: \( R + 2 = M + 2 \).

First, let's express \( H \) in terms of \( R \):
\[ H - 1 = \frac{1}{2}R \]
Multiplying both sides by 2 to clear the fraction, we get:
\[ 2H - 2 = R \]
So,
\[ R = 2H - 2 \]

Next, substitute \( R \) into the equation for \( H \):
\[ H - 1 = \frac{1}{2}(2H - 2) \]
Simplify the right side:
\[ H - 1 = H - 1 \]
This is an identity, so it doesn't give us new information about \( H \). Instead, let's use the second condition \( H - 1 = \frac{1}{2}R \) directly.

Now, let's find \( R \):
\[ R = 2H - 2 \]
We need to find \( H \) such that this relationship holds true. Since \( R \) must be a positive integer, let's test some values for \( H \):

If \( H = 5 \):
\[ R = 2(5) - 2 = 8 \]
Check if \( H - 1 = \frac{1}{2}R \):
\[ 5 - 1 = \frac{1}{2} \times 8 \]
\[ 4 = 4 \]
This is true, so \( H = 5 \) and \( R = 8 \).
