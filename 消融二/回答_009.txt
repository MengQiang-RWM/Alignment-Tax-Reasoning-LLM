1. **Initial number of apples:**
   - Xiao Ming has 3 apples.

2. **Apples for Xiao Hong:**
   - Xiao Hong has 2 more apples than Xiao Ming.
     \[
     \text{Number of apples Xiao Hong has} = 3 + 2 = 5
     \]

3. **Apples for Xiao Hua:**
   - Xiao Hua has half as many apples as Xiao Hong.
     \[
     \text{Number of apples Xiao Hua has} = \frac{5}{2} = 2.5
     \]
   - Since we can't have a fraction of an apple in real life, we round it to the nearest whole number, which is 2.5 apples. However, typically in such problems, we consider the contextually appropriate value, so we'll say Xiao Hua has 2 or 3 apples depending on how he distributes them among his friends.

4. **Apples given to Xiao Ming:**
   - Xiao Hua gives 1 apple to Xiao Ming.
   - Therefore, the new number of apples Xiao Ming has is:
     \[
     \text{New number of apples Xiao Ming has} = 3 + 1 = 4
     \]
