Let's break down the problem step by step:

1. **Initial number of apples:**
   - Xiao Ming has 3 apples.
   - Xiao Hong has 2 more apples than Xiao Ming.
     \[
     \text{Number of apples Xiao Hong has} = 3 + 2 = 5
     \]

2. **Apples given by Xiao Hua:**
   - Xiao Hua has half as many apples as Xiao Hong.
     \[
     \text{Number of apples Xiao Hua has} = \frac{5}{2}
     \]
   - Since we're dealing with whole apples, let's assume Xiao Hua receives 2 apples (as it is reasonable to give away half an apple).

3. **Apples left with Xiao Ming after giving some to Xiao Hua:**
   - After receiving 2 apples from Xiao Hua:
     \[
     \text{Remaining apples for Xiao Ming} = 3 - 2 = 1
     \]

Therefore, Xiao Ming now has 1 apple. This conclusion is based on the logical deduction that if Xiao Hua gave 2 apples to Xiao Ming, he would be left with half of his original amount of apples, which in this case is \( \frac{5}{2} \) or 2.5 apples. Since we can't have half an apple in real life, the practical interpretation here is that Xiao Ming received exactly 2 apples, leaving him with 1 apple.
