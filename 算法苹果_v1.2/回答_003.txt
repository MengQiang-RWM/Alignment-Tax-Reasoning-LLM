Let's break down the problem step by step:

1. **Initial number of apples:**
   - Xiao Ming has 3 apples.
   - Xiao Hong has 2 more apples than Xiao Ming.
     \[
     \text{Apples Xiao Hong has} = 3 + 2 = 5
     \]
   - Therefore, Xiao Hong has 5 apples.

2. **Total initial apples:**
   - Adding up all the apples:
     \[
     \text{Total initial apples} = 3 (\text{Xiao Ming}) + 5 (\text{Xiao Hong}) = 8
     \]

3. **Apples given by Xiao Hua:**
   - Xiao Hua has half as many apples as Xiao Hong.
     \[
     \text{Apples Xiao Hua has} = \frac{5}{2} = 2.5
     \]
   - Since we can't give half an apple in this context, let's assume Xiao Hua gives 2 apples (as it is common practice to give whole apples).

4. **Apples remaining with Xiao Ming after giving 2 apples:**
   - Subtracting the apples given by Xiao Hua from his total:
     \[
     \text{Remaining apples with Xiao Ming} = 8 - 2 = 6
     \]

Therefore, Xiao Ming has 6 apples now. The final answer is:
\[
\boxed{6}
